Calculate risk

The probability of 90-day kidney recovery was estimated using a Cox proportional hazards model. The linear predictor from the model, indicating the risk of a patient to recover kidney function by 90-days, is calculated as follows: $$ \begin{align*} \text{LP} &= -2.535 \\ & -0.0130 \cdot \text{Age in years}\\ & + 0.5077 \cdot \text{I(Male)} \\ & -0.5656 \cdot \text{I(History of congestive heart failure)} \\ & -0.4701 \cdot \text{I(Hypertension requiring 3 or more medications)} \\ & +0.3159 \cdot \text{Serum Albumin (g/dL)} \\ & -0.4088 \cdot \text{Pre-dialysis Serum Creatinine (mg/dL)} \\ & +0.0214 \cdot \text{(Pre-dialysis Serum Creatinine (mg/dL))^2} \\ & -0.0011 \cdot \text{Parathyroid hormone (pg/mL)} \\ & +0.0016 \cdot \text{Platelet count (x10E3/uL)} \\ & -0.3300 \cdot \text{Serum Potassium (mmol/L)} \\ & -0.1244 \cdot \text{Interdialytic weight change (kg)} \\ \end{align*} $$ where \begin{equation} \text{I(condition)} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if condition is true} \\ 0 & \text{if condition is false} \end{cases} \end{equation} Note, interdialytic weight change should be ascertained at the time closest to but not following ascertainment of variables measured only once in the first week. Furthermore, if platelet count is for example 100.000uL, one should enter 100.

Probability of kidney recovery

The probability of 90-day kidney function recovery is calculated by: $$ \begin{align*} \text{hazard} &= 0.377 \cdot \exp(0.935 \cdot \text{LP}) \\ P(\text{Kidney recovery within 90 days}) &= \exp(-\text{hazard}) \cdot 100 \end{align*} $$ where hazard is the hazard of 90-day kidney function recovery, exp is the natural exponential function, LP is the linear predictor calculated above, and P is probability.
